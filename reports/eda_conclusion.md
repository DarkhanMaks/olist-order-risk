# EDA conclusion

The hypothesis is partly confirmed. Delivery delay is the strongest signal: orders delivered 3+ days late get a bad review 61-79% of the time, against 9-11% for orders that arrive early (overall rate 12.8%). The rate jumps sharply between "late 0-3d" (19%) and "late 3-7d" (61%), so a few days of grace barely hurts but a real delay almost always does. Total shipping time also matters, but only in the slowest decile (44% bad reviews vs 7-14% elsewhere), and much of that overlaps with lateness. Freight cost does not support the hypothesis: the bad-review rate is flat (12-14%) across all freight-to-price deciles. These are associations, not causes; late orders are also slow, so the model has to separate their effects.
